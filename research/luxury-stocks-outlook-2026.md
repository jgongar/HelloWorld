# Luxury Consumption Trends & Outlook for French and Italian Luxury Stocks

*Study date: 17 October 2026. Figures come from company releases and the press reports cited at the end. Scenario probabilities and return ranges are my own judgement. This is research, not investment advice.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Luxury is in its longest downturn in decades, and it is not over yet.** The personal luxury goods market fell to **€358bn in 2025** (from €364bn in 2024). Bain-Altagamma's base case for 2026 is only **+2–4%** (€365–373bn), and Q3 2026 data show the recovery slowing again: LVMH's Fashion & Leather Goods division fell **–5% organic** against a consensus of about –2%. Over the last 30 years luxury slumps have rarely lasted more than two years. This one is entering its third, which tells you part of the problem is **structural, not only cyclical**.
2. **The 2021–2024 growth model ran out of road.** Most of the industry's growth came from **price increases, not volume**. Aspirational and middle-class buyers left (Bain estimated about 50 million fewer luxury customers by 2024), and brands stopped innovating. Goldman Sachs now blames the slowdown more on "aggressive pricing and a slower pace of innovation" than on macro factors.
3. **Demand has polarised (K-shaped).** Brands that serve the very wealthy keep growing: Hermès (+6% at constant currency in H1), Brunello Cucinelli (about +12% FY guidance), Ferrari (order book full through 2027) and Richemont's jewellery (+24% in its June quarter). Brands that depend on aspirational buyers are stuck: Gucci, Louis Vuitton's entry products, Dior.
4. **The geographic engine has moved from China to the US.** The Americas are the strongest region at almost every house (Hermès +15%, Prada +17% organic, Gucci US +9%, LVMH US +5% in Q3). China is in a structural reset: global Chinese luxury spending fell 13% in 2025, and the mainland weakened again in Q3 2026. The Middle East fell sharply during the **US–Iran war** (Feb–June 2026) and should rebound mechanically in 2027. Europe relies on tourists and is soft.
5. **Spending is shifting between categories.** Jewellery is beating leather goods and fashion. Experiences (hospitality, travel, yachts, art) grow about 4% a year, faster than goods. Eyewear has a real technology driver: EssilorLuxottica's Meta AI glasses nearly doubled in Q2.
6. **Valuations have already de-rated a lot.** European luxury lost roughly **€300bn of market value in 2026**, and the sector was down close to a quarter year-to-date by September. It now trades at about **25x forward earnings, near its 10-year average**. Its premium over the STOXX 600 is at the low end of the post-Covid range. Dispersion between stocks is very high.
7. **Outlook (my view):** a **gradual, uneven recovery in 2027–2028** is the most likely path (about 55%). Organic growth returns to around **4–6%**, operating leverage lifts EPS by high-single to low-double digits, and the stocks deliver roughly **8–12% a year** in total return with stable multiples. **Stock picking will matter more than sector exposure.**

### 2.2 The latest data points

By region: US +5%, Europe +4%, Asia excluding Japan –5%. - **Kering, H1 2026:** €7.22bn, +1% comparable. **Q2 was +2%, the first growth in three years.** Gucci Q2 –2% (consensus –4.7%) with Gucci US +9%. Recurring operating margin 12.8%. Net debt down to **€3.3bn from €8.0bn** after selling Kering Beauté to L'Oréal and a Milan property deal. Management expects Q3 to be roughly flat.
- **Prada, H1 2026:** €3.05bn, +16% at constant currency, **+5% organic (Q2 +7%)**. Americas +17% organic, Europe –4%, Middle East –24%. Versace added €305m. EBIT fell to €523m from €607m because Versace dilutes margins.
- **Moncler, H1 2026:** €1.29bn, **+9% at constant currency**, slowing to +5% in Q2 (Moncler brand Q2 +3%). Asia +19%, EMEA –4%, Stone Island +11%. EBIT margin 19.0%; €1.1bn net cash. The group changed its governance in July.
- **Brunello Cucinelli, 9M 2026:** about €1.12bn (about +10% reported). Q3 €374m, slightly above consensus. FY guidance reportedly raised to **about +12% at constant currency** (from 10–11%), and about +10% expected for 2027.
- **Ferrari, Q2 2026:** €1.94bn (+8.4%), EBIT margin 31.2%. FY guidance raised to about €7.6bn revenue and EPS of at least €9.68. **The order book covers all of 2027.**
- **EssilorLuxottica, H1 2026:** €14.8bn, **+9.7% at constant currency**. Meta AI glasses sales nearly doubled in Q2, the myopia-control range grew 24%, and adjusted operating profit rose 15% at constant currency.
- **Richemont (Swiss, used as a benchmark):** FY to March 2026 +11% at constant currency. June quarter **+20%**, with jewellery +24% and the Americas +27%. This shows clearly how strong hard luxury is.

## 3. Structural trends in luxury consumption

### Trend 1: The end of "luxflation"
Between 2019 and 2024 the big houses raised prices by a cumulative 50–100% on iconic products, well above inflation. That created a **value-for-money gap**: quality and creativity did not rise with price, and aspirational shoppers walked away. Price increases are no longer an easy growth lever. In 2026 brands are leaning instead on **mix, new products and entry categories** (small leather goods, beauty, eyewear). A 15% US tariff on EU goods (in force since August 2025) has forced some selective price increases in the US: Hermès passed the tariff through fully. That works for scarcity brands and is risky for aspirational ones.

**Implication:** the gap between brands with real pricing power (Hermès, Cucinelli, Ferrari, Cartier) and the rest will widen.

### Trend 2: Polarisation between top clients and aspirational buyers
A small group of top clients (roughly the top 2–5% by spend) generates a disproportionate share of sales. They keep spending, helped by financial-asset wealth (US equities and AI-driven market gains). The aspirational middle class in China, Europe and among younger US buyers has cut back. "Quiet luxury" and understated design (Cucinelli, Loro Piana, Hermès, The Row) beat logo-heavy products.

**Implication:** businesses serving ultra-high-net-worth clients are more resilient. The risk is that this demand depends on asset prices, so **a sharp equity-market correction is the biggest single threat to the top end.**

### Trend 3: Rotation from soft to hard luxury, and from goods to experiences
- **Jewellery** is the best-performing category. It is seen as a store of value (gold at record highs), it does not go out of fashion, and buyers in the US and China see it as an investment.
- **Experiences** (hospitality, dining, travel, wellness) grow faster than goods. Shopify research quoted in the press says 88% of high-income consumers define status through experiences and values rather than logos.
- **Beauty and fragrance** are mixed: still the "entry ticket" to luxury, but slowing (Hermès perfume and beauty –4%).

**Implication:** groups with large jewellery businesses (LVMH with Tiffany and Bulgari; Kering's planned jewellery push) gain support. Pure fashion and leather businesses carry more cyclical risk.

### Trend 4: Geography, from China to the US (and the Middle East as a swing factor)

| Region | Current state | 2027–2030 outlook |
|---|---|---|
| **US** | Main engine; double-digit growth at many houses; supported by asset-driven wealth | Should keep outperforming; risks are tariffs and a stock-market correction |
| **Mainland China** | Structural reset: property deflation, youth unemployment, "common prosperity" and anti-ostentation, rising local brands (Laopu Gold, Songmont); 40% of consumers plan to cut luxury spending (AlixPartners) | Stabilisation, not a V-shaped rebound; growth may resume at low single digits; Hainan duty-free is partly policy-driven |
| **Chinese travellers abroad** | Shrinking; more purchases moving back to the mainland and Hainan | Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia gain; Europe loses |
| **Middle East** | Shock during the US–Iran war (Dubai mall sales –30–50% in March; Prada region –24% in H1); peace deal in June | **Mechanical rebound in 2027** from an easy comparison base |
| **Europe** | Driven by tourists (US visitors strong, Chinese weak); a strong euro discourages purchases | Muted; depends on US tourists |
| **Japan** | Weak yen attracts tourist shopping | Volatile, depends on currency |
| **India / Southeast Asia** | Small but fast-growing | A long-term growth option, not yet material |

### Trend 5: Generational change
Gen Z and Gen Alpha will make up a growing share of buyers (Bain has estimated up to about 30% of purchases by 2030). They are:
- **More value- and story-driven:** they buy into brands with cultural relevance and a strong narrative. Miu Miu was the clearest example in 2023–25, though it is now cooling.
- **Open to resale:** the second-hand market makes durable icons more valuable (Hermès bags, Rolex, Cartier) and penalises trend-driven products.
- **Digital-first, but they still value the store experience.**
- **More attentive to ESG and supply chains.** Italian courts placed units of Dior, Armani, Valentino and Loro Piana under judicial administration in 2024–25 over subcontractor labour conditions, which damaged trust in "Made in Italy" craftsmanship claims.

### Trend 6: A creative reset (the main 2027 catalyst)
2025–2026 saw the biggest turnover of creative directors in a generation: Demna at Gucci, Jonathan Anderson at Dior, Matthieu Blazy at Chanel, Pierpaolo Piccioli at Balenciaga, new teams at Loewe, Celine and Versace. **2026–27 is when their collections fully reach stores.** Gucci's new bags (Borsetto, Paparazzo) are already lifting US sales. If the industry's problem is mainly a lack of innovation, as Goldman argues, then the new product cycle is the most important recovery driver.

### Trend 7: Consolidation and balance-sheet clean-up
- **Prada bought Versace** (closed December 2025), an Italian consolidation play.
- **Kering sold its beauty business to L'Oréal**, cut net debt by more than half, and is closing about 100 stores.
- **Armani** must sell a 15% stake under the founder's will. L'Oréal, LVMH and EssilorLuxottica are cited as possible buyers, with a possible later majority sale or IPO.
- Further deals are likely: Italian family houses face succession questions, and French groups have firepower.

### Trend 8: Currency and policy headwinds
- **A strong euro** is cutting 3–5 pp from reported growth (Hermès +6.1% at constant currency vs +1.6% reported; LVMH –3% reported vs +2% organic).
- **US tariffs** of 15% on EU goods.
- **French fiscal risk:** an exceptional surtax on large companies' profits (it held back Hermès' net profit) and rising French bond yields hit French luxury shares in September 2026.

## 4. Company analysis

### 4.1 French stocks

#### LVMH (MC FP): cyclical recovery with real value
- **Strengths:** the most diversified portfolio (fashion, jewellery, Sephora, wines and spirits); strong balance sheet (net debt €8.25bn); stable 22.5% margin; growth businesses in Sephora and jewellery.
- **Weaknesses:** Louis Vuitton is fatigued and Dior's recovery is slower than expected. It has high China exposure. Wines and spirits face weak cognac demand and tariff disputes.
- **Valuation:** reportedly about 25% below its 10-year average multiple.
- **View:** **the best "recovery" vehicle among the mega-caps.** If Dior's new creative direction and LV's product pipeline work in 2027, this stock re-rates first. It needs proof that Fashion & Leather Goods can return to growth. Q4 2026 and FY results in January 2027 are the key test.

#### Hermès (RMS FP): the best business, expensive but de-rated
- **Strengths:** the strongest pricing power in the sector (41% operating margin); waiting lists; growing even in a weak market (+6% at constant currency); serves the very wealthy.
- **Weaknesses:** the share price fell about 27% year-to-date by early September and has lost about half its peak market value, even though earnings have not fallen. That shows how much of the stock was pure valuation premium. Goldman rates it Sell on valuation.
- **View:** **fundamentally the safest stock in the sector**, but it is a story of multiple normalisation. Expect returns close to its earnings growth (about 8–10% a year). It will behave as a defensive holding in the bear case and lag in a strong cyclical rally.

#### Kering (KER FP): high-risk turnaround
- **Context:** revenue fell 10% in 2025 to €14.7bn and recurring operating profit fell 33%. The margin collapsed from 27.5% in 2022 to 11.1% in 2025.
- **Positives:** new CEO Luca de Meo has a credible plan: reduce reliance on Gucci, push jewellery, close stores, and more than double the margin over time. Net debt is down to €3.3bn. **Q2 2026 brought the first growth in three years**, and Gucci beat expectations.
- **Risks:** Q3 guidance is "flat", the US comparison base is tough, and the stock trades at more than 40x earnings on depressed profits. Gucci has fallen for 12 consecutive quarters.
- **View:** **the highest-beta option on a sector recovery.** If Gucci returns to growth in 2027 and the margin heads back toward 20%, the stock could double. If the turnaround stalls, it could fall another 30%. Q3 sales (late October) are a key binary event.

#### EssilorLuxottica (EL FP): structural growth (Franco-Italian)
- **Strengths:** eyewear is less tied to the economic cycle (prescription glasses are a necessity); a medical business (myopia control +24%); **Meta AI glasses** as a new technology category (more than 7 million units in 2025, sales nearly doubling).
- **Risks:** dependence on Meta; competition from Google, Samsung and Apple in smart glasses; premium valuation.
- **View:** **the most structural growth story in the sector** and the least exposed to China or aspirational demand. An attractive core holding.

#### Other French-linked names
- **L'Oréal (Luxe division):** now holds the Kering Beauté brands; beauty is slowing but still the entry door to luxury.
- **Christian Dior SE:** a holding-company proxy for LVMH, trading at a discount.

### 4.2 Italian stocks

#### Prada (1913 HK): value and execution
- **Strengths:** consistent execution; Prada brand accelerating (Q2 +6.3%); Americas very strong; Versace offers multi-year upside if management repeats the turnaround it achieved with Miu Miu.
- **Weaknesses:** Miu Miu has slowed sharply (+2.5% against a +60% comparison base); Versace dilutes margins (EBIT fell from €607m to €523m); the Middle East fell 24%.
- **Valuation:** historically trades at a large discount to its 10-year average (Barclays estimated –53% on a two-year forward P/E). Goldman rates it Buy.
- **View:** **the best "value plus self-help" Italian story.** A Versace turnaround is the main driver for 2027–2030.

#### Moncler (MONC IM): good brand, uncertain near term
- **Strengths:** strong in China and Asia (+19%), €1.1bn net cash, Stone Island growing; Goldman rates it Buy.
- **Weaknesses:** Q2 slowed to +5% and the Moncler brand to +3%. EMEA is weak because tourism is soft. Governance changed in July 2026. **Q4 (the outerwear season) and the weather decide the year.**
- **View:** **neutral or watch.** It becomes attractive if Q3 and Q4 confirm that China is holding up and Europe stabilises.

#### Brunello Cucinelli (BC IM): the top-tier compounder
- **Strengths:** about 10–12% growth every year; serves ultra-high-net-worth clients; "quiet luxury" positioning; strong visibility (guides about +10% for 2027).
- **Weaknesses:** a very high valuation leaves no room for disappointment; it is a small-cap with limited liquidity.
- **View:** **a defensive quality holding** where returns roughly track earnings growth. Best bought on market sell-offs.

#### Ferrari (RACE IM): scarcity model, slower growth
- **Strengths:** order book full through 2027; 31% EBIT margin; personalisation drives mix; 2026 guidance raised; €7bn shareholder-return plan for 2026–2030.
- **Weaknesses:** the October 2025 Capital Markets Day implied slower growth (about 5% revenue CAGR to 2030, about €9bn in 2030) and the stock fell 16% in one day. The first electric Ferrari is an execution risk.
- **View:** **a quality defensive holding**, but its "luxury compounder" premium has partly faded. Expect returns roughly in line with earnings plus buybacks (about 8–10% a year).

#### Other Italian names
- **Zegna (NYSE):** a niche menswear and Tom Ford play.
- **Salvatore Ferragamo:** a long, uncertain turnaround; speculative.
- **Armani (private):** stake sale under way and a possible IPO.
- **Tod's, Dolce & Gabbana, Valentino:** private or delisted.

## 5. Valuation

- **Sector:** about **25x forward P/E**, near its 10-year average, after a large de-rating (the combined market value of LVMH, Hermès, Kering and Richemont is roughly half its 2024 peak, according to press reports).
- **Premium to the STOXX 600:** back to the 10-year average, at the low end of the post-Covid range.
- **Dispersion:** Hermès, Cucinelli and Ferrari still trade at large premiums. Prada and LVMH are below their own historical averages, and Kering only looks expensive because its profits are depressed.
- **Historical signal:** JPMorgan found that after US consumer sentiment (University of Michigan index) bottoms, European luxury has historically **outperformed the market by 9–12%**.

**Bottom line:** the sector is not "cheap" in absolute terms, but much of the bad news is priced in. **Further gains need earnings to recover, not a higher multiple.**

## 6. Scenarios 2027–2030

| | **Bear case (25%)** | **Base case (55%)** | **Bull case (20%)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Narrative** | China deflation persists, a US equity correction hits top-end clients, the Middle East flares up again, the euro stays strong | Gradual recovery: US resilient, Middle East rebounds, China stabilises, the creative cycle starts to work | Synchronised recovery: China rebounds, the US stays strong, new collections succeed, the euro weakens |
| **Personal luxury market 2027** | 0% to +2% | +4% to +6% | +7% to +9% |
| **Sector EPS 2027** | –5% to 0% | +8% to +12% | +15% to +20% |
| **Forward P/E** | 18–20x | 22–25x | 27–30x |
| **Stock returns, 12–24 months** | –15% to –25% | +8% to +12% a year | +30% to +50% |
| **Relative winners** | Hermès, Cucinelli, Ferrari, EssilorLuxottica | LVMH, Prada, EssilorLuxottica, Hermès | Kering, LVMH, Moncler, Prada |
| **Relative losers** | Kering, LVMH, Moncler | Moncler (if China slows), Kering (if Gucci stalls) | Hermès, Cucinelli, Ferrari (lag in a rally) |

### Longer-term view (2030)
- **Structural growth:** I expect personal luxury to grow at about **4–6% a year** over the long term (roughly nominal global GDP plus 1–2 pp), well below the 2021–2023 boom.
- **Fewer but richer clients:** the industry will depend more on the top client segment. Pricing power will be selective.
- **Jewellery, eyewear and experiences will outgrow fashion and leather goods.**
- **The US becomes the main market**, China returns as a moderate-growth market, and India becomes a credible third pillar after 2030.
- **More consolidation:** Italian family houses (Armani, Dolce & Gabbana, Etro and others) will be absorbed by groups, mainly French ones.
- **The winners will be the brands with real scarcity and craftsmanship credibility.** Brands that over-extended into aspirational volume will struggle to grow.

## 7. French vs Italian stocks compared

| Factor | French luxury | Italian luxury |
|---|---|---|
| **Size and liquidity** | Mega-caps, CAC 40 heavyweights | Mid/small-caps (except Ferrari) |
| **China exposure** | High (LVMH, Kering) | Mixed (Moncler high; Cucinelli, Ferrari low) |
| **Positioning** | Full spectrum, from accessible to ultra-luxury | More niche and ultra-high-end (Cucinelli, Ferrari, Loro Piana-type positioning) |
| **Country risk** | French fiscal and political risk, profit surtax, bond yields | Supply-chain scrutiny (labour investigations) |
| **Corporate actions** | Acquirers (LVMH, Kering, L'Oréal, EssilorLuxottica) | Targets or consolidators (Armani, Prada–Versace) |
| **Volatility** | Moves with China and macro data | Moves more on company-specific factors |
| **Best for** | Exposure to a cyclical recovery (LVMH, Kering) | Exposure to quality and the top client segment (Cucinelli, Ferrari), or value (Prada) |

**Conclusion:** a balanced portfolio would combine French **cyclical recovery** names (LVMH, plus a small, risk-controlled Kering position) with Italian **quality and value** names (Cucinelli, Ferrari, Prada), anchored by **EssilorLuxottica** as structural growth and **Hermès** as a defensive core holding.

## 9. Main risks to this view

1. **US equity correction:** the wealth effect reverses and the top client segment slows.
2. **China deflation deepens**, or geopolitical tension escalates (Taiwan, sanctions, boycotts).
3. **The Middle East conflict resumes.**
4. **The euro stays strong**, cutting another 3–5 pp from reported growth.
5. **The new creative directors fail**, which would mean the innovation problem was not the main issue.
6. **Brand dilution** from over-pricing, or supply-chain or ESG scandals.
7. **Tariff escalation** between the US and EU.

- Some figures differ between press sources (for example, Gucci Q2 is reported as –2% or –4%). I used the company releases where available.
- The Cucinelli guidance raise to about 12% comes from a single source (Smartkarma) and should be checked against the company's October release.

- Q3 2026 results for Hermès, Kering, Prada, Moncler and EssilorLuxottica had not yet been published on the study date.
